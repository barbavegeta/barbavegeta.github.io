MSc dissertation · Statistical genetics

GPNMB and Parkinson’s disease: Mendelian randomisation and colocalisation

Two-sample MR, LD-aware sensitivity analysis and Bayesian colocalisation to test whether genetically predicted GPNMB protein levels are linked to Parkinson’s disease risk. The evidence supports prioritisation, not causality.

Human genetics · Target discovery · Nextflow

Proteome-wide cis-MR and colocalisation scan for Parkinson’s disease

Scaled the dissertation from one protein to the plasma proteome: a Nextflow pipeline that tests each of ~2,900 UKB-PPP proteins with cis-pQTL MR and colocalisation, then applies FDR control, evidence tiers and an HTML report.

Single-nucleus transcriptomics · Parkinson’s disease

GPNMB in Parkinson’s disease: snRNA-seq re-analysis

Re-analysed 83,484 substantia nigra nuclei from 29 donors to localise GPNMB and test PD–control differences with donor-level pseudobulk models. GPNMB was highest in microglia and about two-fold higher in PD microglia.

FAQ

Have questions?

The things recruiters and hiring managers usually ask first.

What kind of role are you looking for?

Bioinformatics roles in statistical genetics, transcriptomics and NGS analysis, especially where clinical or cell and gene therapy experience helps.

Where are you based? Can you work remotely?

I’m in London and open to hybrid and remote work. I have the right to work in both the United Kingdom and the European Union.

What sets you apart from other bioinformaticians?

I came to bioinformatics from the lab bench: seven years in regulated clinical laboratories, two of them preparing NGS libraries for clinical genetic testing. I know how sequencing data is generated, where it fails QC, and what audit-ready documentation looks like, and that shapes how I analyse it.

How do you approach an analysis?

I fix the primary analysis before looking at the effect I care about, treat donors rather than cells as replicates, and run the sensitivity analyses that could weaken a result. Conclusions go no further than the evidence.

Then I package it: scripts, saved intermediate results, session information and provenance, so someone else can rerun it.

What tools do you use?

Mostly R, Python and Nextflow, with Docker or Conda for environments. For genetics: coloc, SuSiE and OpenGWAS. For single-cell: Scanpy, AnnData and PyDESeq2. For NGS: STAR, Salmon, BWA-MEM2, samtools, bcftools and DESeq2. The full toolkit is on the About page.
