Prioritising GPNMB as a Parkinson's disease-relevant protein using Mendelian randomisation and colocalisation

Integrated two-sample Mendelian randomisation, LD-aware sensitivity analysis, Bayesian colocalisation and transcriptomic evidence to evaluate GPNMB as a Parkinson's disease-relevant target. The SomaScan cis-instrument gave OR 1.32 (95% CI 1.19–1.46; P = 2.6×10−7) and colocalisation supported a shared signal (PP.H4 = 0.94), but heterogeneous Olink instruments meant the conclusion stops at prioritisation rather than causality.

Objective, approach, outcome & figure
  • Objective: Test whether genetically predicted GPNMB protein levels are associated with Parkinson's disease risk and whether the protein and disease associations are consistent with a shared regional genetic signal.
  • Approach: Analysed SomaScan and UKB-PPP Olink pQTL data against Parkinson's disease GWAS summary statistics using instrument QC, LD clumping, Wald-ratio and IVW MR, Cochran's Q, multiplicative random-effects sensitivity analysis, coloc.abf, SuSiE and cross-tissue eQTL support.
  • Outcome: SomaScan rs5850 Wald ratio OR 1.32 (95% CI 1.19–1.46); coloc.abf PP.H4 = 0.94 across 150 SNPs. UKB-PPP Olink instruments (5–25 after clumping) showed strong heterogeneity (Cochran’s Q p ≈ 10−5) and non-significant random-effects estimates (strict set OR 1.08, 95% CI 0.88–1.33), and prior-sensitivity and SuSiE results showed the colocalisation depends on locus structure. The final interpretation therefore prioritises GPNMB for further study rather than treating the evidence as causal closure.
  • Reproducibility: Packaged analysis scripts, saved instrument sets, derived results, figure-generation code, software session information and source-provenance records so the reported analyses can be audited and reproduced.

Human genetics · Target discovery · Nextflow

Proteome-wide cis-MR and Colocalisation Scan for Parkinson's Disease

Scaled my dissertation from one protein to the plasma proteome: a Nextflow pipeline that tests each of ~2,900 UKB-PPP proteins as a cause of Parkinson's disease with cis-pQTL Mendelian randomisation and colocalisation, then applies FDR control, evidence tiers and an HTML report.

Details
  • Approach: Per-protein parallel tasks; allele harmonisation across GRCh38 pQTL and GRCh37 GWAS coordinates; LD clumping on 1000 Genomes EUR; Wald ratio, IVW (fixed and random effects), MR-Egger and weighted median; approximate Bayes factor colocalisation with prior sensitivity; Benjamini–Hochberg FDR.
  • Validation: The Python implementation reproduces my dissertation's R results (coloc.abf, IVW with Cochran's Q, Wald ratio) to at least eight significant figures. 14 automated tests and a test profile in which the real GPNMB locus and four simulated proteins each land in their expected evidence tier.
  • Design choice: Loci where single-variant colocalisation favours distinct signals but PD has a genome-wide significant hit are flagged for multi-signal follow-up rather than discarded, as with GPNMB.

Single-nucleus transcriptomics · Parkinson's disease

GPNMB in Parkinson's Disease - snRNA-seq Re-analysis

Re-analysed 83,484 substantia nigra nuclei from 29 donors (GSE243639) to localise GPNMB expression and test PD–control differences with donor-level pseudobulk modelling. GPNMB was highest in microglia and about 2-fold higher in PD microglia (log2FC 1.03, P = 0.009), a nominal effect that did not survive transcriptome-wide FDR (0.22).

Details
  • Problem: Cell-level differential expression can overstate evidence when thousands of nuclei from the same donor are treated as independent biological replicates.
  • Approach: Analysed the author-QC GSE243639 atlas (83,484 nuclei; 29 donors), re-clustered 12,995 microglial nuclei, ran donor-level pseudobulk DE with age and sex adjustment, tested composition with Propeller, and performed pathway enrichment plus external transcriptomic validation.
  • Outcome: GPNMB was most strongly expressed in microglia and was approximately two-fold higher in PD microglial pseudobulk (log2FC 1.025; P = 0.00863; FDR = 0.218). Broad microglial abundance was not detectably different between groups, while external analyses showed directionally concordant GPNMB effects with variable statistical strength.

Microbial genomics · WGS

M. tuberculosis WGS Variant Analysis Workflow (Galaxy)

End-to-end Galaxy workflow from SRA reads to annotated resistance variants, run on 6 clinical M. tuberculosis isolates. It called rpoB (S450L, H445R, D435Y, L430P) and katG (S315T, Q295*) mutations, confirmed in IGV, and classified 4 isolates as genotypically MDR-TB and 2 as rifampicin-resistant.

Details
  • Problem: Resistance interpretation from WGS data requires a long multi-step workflow with multiple failure points and high risk of inconsistent analysis.
  • Action: Built and executed a Galaxy-based workflow covering QC, trimming, alignment, coverage analysis, variant calling, annotation, and IGV-based validation.
  • Result: 98–99.6% of reads mapped, with at least 52× depth across every target locus, so absent calls could be interpreted as true negatives rather than coverage gaps. Variant tables, resistance summary and IGV evidence are in the repository.

Transcriptomics · Pipelines

RNA-seq Pipeline (Nextflow + Docker)

Samplesheet-driven Nextflow DSL2 pipeline from raw FASTQ to differential expression in one command: FastQC, Cutadapt, then STAR + featureCounts or Salmon, MultiQC and DESeq2 with any design formula, all in versioned Docker containers. Validated on real human data: 8 of 8 known dexamethasone-response genes recovered.

Details
  • Problem: RNA-seq analyses are often assembled by hand, depend on one machine's environment and are hard to reproduce.
  • Action: Built a containerised Nextflow pipeline with two quantification routes: STAR genome alignment with fragment-level featureCounts, and low-memory Salmon with gene-level summarisation. DESeq2 takes a configurable design formula and an explicit reference level, so paired or blocked designs are supported.
  • Validation: Ran on the public airway dataset (GSE52778; 4 donors, dexamethasone vs control, paired ~ cell + condition design). Salmon mapped 94–95% of fragments and DESeq2 found 615 differentially expressed genes. All 8 canonical glucocorticoid-response genes came out strongly up (e.g. FKBP5, ZBTB16, PER1, DUSP1; padj < 1e-9). The whole run took under 10 minutes on a 16 GB laptop. A small bundled test dataset runs in GitHub Actions on every push.

Airway smooth muscle cells, dexamethasone vs control: 350 genes up and 265 down (padj < 0.05, |log2FC| > 1), known targets labelled

Regulatory genomics · Machine learning

RegulonML - Predicting Regulatory Variant Effects in Unseen Promoters and Enhancers

Predicted the effects of 41,724 single-base variants in 21 promoters and enhancers from DNA sequence alone (saturation-mutagenesis MPRA), scoring transcription-factor binding-site gain and loss for 1,019 JASPAR motifs and testing only on loci the model had never seen.

Details
  • Problem: The first version used read counts that the target is computed from and split by dataset label, so the same DNA appeared in training and test. Under a proper split every model did worse than predicting the mean.
  • Approach: Rebuilt each element's sequence from the mutagenesis data, scored binding-site creation and loss for every SNV and deletion on both strands (vectorised, checked against brute force), and evaluated ridge and gradient-boosting models with leave-one-locus-out cross-validation.
  • Outcome: Motif features doubled the held-out correlation (median Spearman ρ 0.07 → 0.13; AUROC 0.60 for significant variants). A random split inflates ρ to 0.31, quantifying the leakage. Without any training, the motif scoring recovers the ETS-site creation behind the TERT C228T/C250T cancer mutations.
