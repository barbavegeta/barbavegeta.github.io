Prioritising GPNMB as a Parkinson's disease-relevant protein using Mendelian randomisation and colocalisation

Integrated two-sample Mendelian randomisation, LD-aware sensitivity analysis, Bayesian colocalisation and transcriptomic evidence to evaluate GPNMB as a Parkinson's disease-relevant target while explicitly testing heterogeneity and locus-level uncertainty.

Objective, approach, outcome & figure
  • Objective: Test whether genetically predicted GPNMB protein levels are associated with Parkinson's disease risk and whether the protein and disease associations are consistent with a shared regional genetic signal.
  • Approach: Analysed SomaScan and UKB-PPP Olink pQTL data against Parkinson's disease GWAS summary statistics using instrument QC, LD clumping, Wald-ratio and IVW MR, Cochran's Q, multiplicative random-effects sensitivity analysis, coloc.abf, SuSiE and cross-tissue eQTL support.
  • Outcome: SomaScan and primary colocalisation analyses supported GPNMB locus prioritisation, while substantial Olink heterogeneity and sensitivity analyses weakened any claim of definitive causality. The final interpretation therefore prioritises GPNMB for further study rather than treating the evidence as causal closure.
  • Reproducibility: Packaged analysis scripts, saved instrument sets, derived results, figure-generation code, software session information and source-provenance records so the reported analyses can be audited and reproduced.

Single-nucleus transcriptomics · Parkinson's disease

GPNMB in Parkinson's Disease - snRNA-seq Re-analysis

Re-analysed human substantia nigra single-nucleus RNA-seq data to localise GPNMB expression, test PD-control differences with donor-aware pseudobulk modelling, assess cell-type composition, and compare the signal with independent published microglial analyses.

Details
  • Problem: Cell-level differential expression can overstate evidence when thousands of nuclei from the same donor are treated as independent biological replicates.
  • Approach: Analysed the author-QC GSE243639 atlas (83,484 nuclei; 29 donors), re-clustered 12,995 microglial nuclei, ran donor-level pseudobulk DE with age and sex adjustment, tested composition with Propeller, and performed pathway enrichment plus external transcriptomic validation.
  • Outcome: GPNMB was most strongly expressed in microglia and was approximately two-fold higher in PD microglial pseudobulk (log2FC 1.025; P = 0.00863; FDR = 0.218). Broad microglial abundance was not detectably different between groups, while external analyses showed directionally concordant GPNMB effects with variable statistical strength.
